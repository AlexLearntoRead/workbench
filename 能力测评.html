8. 口读流利度测试
MASI-R Oral Reading Fluency Measures
Grade 1 — Passage 1A: Part of the Park
Part of the park is for play. Some parts are not for play. Cars drive down the street. Don't play there. People walk on the sidewalks. Don't play there. Pick a part of the park for play. There are many pretty places. People play ball. Some people just sit. Some people read books. Dogs run after birds. There are green trees. Pick a part like that.
Grade 1 — Passage 1B: The Mouse
One day a mouse came to our house. She was in a box. That box was her home. She ate there and went to sleep there. She was a little mouse with a little house. A cat lives at our house too. The cat saw the mouse. He sat still. He did not move. The mouse did not run. The cat looked hard at the mouse. "I will eat you, mouse," the cat was thinking. The mouse was thinking too. "I see you, cat, and you are bad news!" I took the mouse to my room. No more cats for this mouse!
Grade 1 — Passage 1C: My Dog
My dog waits by the door. He waits for me to let him out. He wants to go out and play. I look outside. It is fall. Some trees are yellow and some trees are red. Some trees are still green. We will go out and play. We like to play by the trees. My dog runs by the big trees. He is happy and I am happy too. We run and run. Then we rest. I tell him things. He must not run into the street. There may be cars. He listens to me and he stays with me. He is my dog.
Grade 2 — Passage 2B: Ben's Party
Last Sunday afternoon, there was a party for Ben. First, Mom baked a big cake. She fixed something for us to drink and we put the food in a pretty basket. We put in some balloons too. Ben likes balloons. Then I got Ben and we were ready to go! We walked to a place with big trees all around. It was a forest, I think. We looked and looked. At last, we found a nice spot with green grass. Ben rested and we fixed a place for the party. Mom poured some milk for me. I blew up a big red balloon for Ben. We sang "Happy Birthday" with loud, funny voices. We ate white cake with pink icing and it was good! Then Mom asked some crazy riddles. I told her some riddles from school. We laughed and played some more games. Then it was time for Ben's presents. Mom gave him a new blanket with soft insides. I gave him a new blue hat and lots of balloons. We both gave him birthday hugs. We walked home happy. Ben was happy too, but he was tired. I put him on the bed upstairs. That bear had some birthday!
Grade 2 — Passage 2C: The Mountain
One day Dad and I went to the mountain. We saw a big hill. It looked like a big brown cake. We climbed up and up. At the top, we could see all the way to town. We ate our lunch on top of the mountain. Dad had an apple and I had a cookie. We looked down at the cars. They looked like little toy cars. We saw our house too. It looked tiny! On the way down, we saw a little rabbit. It ran fast into the woods. It was a fun day. I want to go back again soon.
Grade 3 — Passage 3A: The Motorcycle
Last summer, my uncle bought a motorcycle. It was big and black. I wanted to ride on it. "Can I go with you?" I asked. Uncle smiled. "You can sit behind me. Hold on tight!" he said. We went up the road. The wind blew in my face. It felt good. We went past the park. We went past the school. We went all the way to the lake. I could see the water shining in the sun. We stopped at a small shop. Uncle bought me a cold drink. "Did you like it?" he asked. "Yes! When can we go again?" I said. He laughed. "Any time!" he said.
Grade 3 — Passage 3B: The Cookies
Mom was making chocolate chip cookies. I was helping her. I stood on a chair by the counter. She let me stir the batter. It was thick and sweet. I wanted to eat some of it. "No," Mom said. "You can't eat raw eggs." I put the cookies on a tray. Mom put them in the oven. Soon the kitchen smelled so good! I watched through the oven window. The cookies turned golden brown. Mom took them out. They were hot. She put icing on top. When they cooled down, I took a bite. It was the best cookie I ever tasted!
Grade 3 — Passage 3C: The Trumpet
My dad used to play the trumpet. He gave it to me. It was shiny and gold. I took it out of the case. It was heavy! Dad showed me how to hold it. I put my mouth on the mouthpiece. I blew hard. Nothing happened. Dad laughed. "You have to buzz your lips," he said. I tried again. This time a sound came out. It was loud and squeaky. My dog ran under the bed. I practiced every day. After a month, I could play a simple song. Dad was proud. "You sound pretty good!" he said.
Grade 4 — Passage 4A: Chess
My grandfather taught me how to play chess. It is a game with two players. Each player has sixteen pieces. There are kings, queens, rooks, bishops, knights, and pawns. The goal is to trap the other player's king. At first, I lost every game. Grandpa was patient. He showed me how each piece moves. "The knight jumps in an L shape," he said. I practiced for weeks. One day, I beat Grandpa! He looked surprised. Then he smiled. "You're getting good," he said. Now we play every Sunday. Chess is hard, but it's fun.
Grade 4 — Passage 4B: The Desert Plant and the Moth
In the desert, there is a plant called the yucca. It has sharp leaves and white flowers. At night, a special moth comes to the flowers. The moth lays its eggs inside the flower. It also puts pollen on the flower. This helps the plant make seeds. When the eggs hatch, the baby moths eat some of the seeds. The plant has enough seeds left to grow new plants. This is called a partnership. The moth and the plant need each other. Neither could live as well without the other.
Grade 4 — Passage 4C: Healthy Lunch
Our school started a new lunch program. Now, every lunch has vegetables. Today we had carrots and peas. We also had fruit. I ate an apple. The bread was whole wheat. The milk was low-fat. "This food is good for you," our teacher said. "It helps you think and play better." I used to hate vegetables. But now, I like them! The cook makes them taste good. She puts butter on the peas. She cooks the carrots until they are sweet. Healthy food doesn't have to taste bad.
Grade 5 — Passage 5A: How Sugar Is Made
Sugar comes from sugar cane. It is a tall grass that grows in warm places. Workers cut the cane. They take it to a factory. There, big machines crush the cane. Juice comes out. The juice is boiled until it becomes thick. Then it is poured into pans. As it cools, crystals form. These crystals are raw sugar. They are brown. Next, the raw sugar is washed. The brown color comes off. Now the sugar is white. It is dried and packaged. Most sugar goes into boxes or bags. It goes to stores all over the world.
Grade 5 — Passage 5B: The Olympics
The Olympic Games started in Greece thousands of years ago. Only men could compete. Women could not even watch. The games stopped for many years. Then in 1896, they started again. Now the Olympics happen every four years. Athletes from all over the world come. They compete in many sports. There are summer games and winter games. The best athletes win medals. Gold is first place. Silver is second. Bronze is third. The Olympics are not just about winning. They are about countries coming together.
Grade 5 — Passage 5C: Toads and Frogs
Toads and frogs look similar, but they are different. Frogs have smooth, wet skin. They live near water. They jump high. Toads have dry, bumpy skin. They live on land more. They walk or take small hops. Both animals eat insects. They have long, sticky tongues. They catch bugs fast. Frogs and toads lay eggs in water. The eggs become tadpoles. Tadpoles have tails and gills. They breathe underwater. As they grow, their legs appear. Their tails disappear. They can breathe air. Then they are adults.
Grade 6 — Passage 6A: Groundhogs
Groundhogs are small animals. They live in holes in the ground. They are also called woodchucks. They eat plants. They can be a problem for farmers. Groundhogs sleep all winter. This is called hibernation. Before winter, they eat a lot. They get fat. Then they go into their burrow. Their heart beats slow. They do not wake up for months. In February, there is a holiday called Groundhog Day. People say that if a groundhog sees its shadow, there will be six more weeks of winter. It's just a fun tradition.
Grade 6 — Passage 6B: The Praying Mantis
The praying mantis is an insect. It gets its name from its front legs. They look like they are praying. The mantis is a hunter. It eats other insects. It can turn its head. No other insect can do that. The mantis waits very still. Its body is green or brown. It looks like a leaf. When another insect comes near, the mantis grabs it. It is fast! The mantis can even fly. It is a useful insect because it eats bugs that hurt plants.
Grade 6 — Passage 6C: Snowflakes
Every snowflake is different. This is true! Snowflakes form in clouds. They start as tiny ice crystals. The crystals grow. They have six sides. Temperature and humidity change their shape. Some snowflakes are simple hexagons. Some have branches. Some look like feathers. No two are exactly alike. You need a microscope to see them well. Scientists have studied snowflakes for years. They still find new shapes. Next time it snows, catch a flake on your glove. Look at it before it melts. You'll see a tiny work of art.
